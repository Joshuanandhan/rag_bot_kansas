THE SALES TAX BASE The sales amount to which sales tax is added is called the sales tax base. The law uses the term gross receipts. Gross receipts is the total selling price or the amount received in money, credits, property or other consideration valued in money. Selling price is the total cost to the consumer, excluding discounts allowed and credited, and excluding delivery charges from retailer to consumer if the delivery charges are separately stated on the invoice. See Delivery, Freight, and Postage, herein. Selling price includes the cost of the property, materials, labor, or service used or expended, losses, overhead, and profit. Other aspects of a sale often affect the selling price or total cost to the consumer. The tax treatment of common elements of a retail sale follows. SALES TAX TREATMENT OF SELECTED TYPES OF SALES AND INCENTIVES Using the previous definition of sales tax base, this section illustrates how to apply state and local sales tax to some common types of sales and sales incentives. Consignment Sales If you receive property from another to sell on consignment, you are the retailer and are responsible for the tax collection rather than the owner of the consigned merchandise. This is true regardless of how you receive your fee or commission. Coupons Beginning July 1, 2024, coupons used by consumers — store coupons or manufacturer coupons — are treated the same for sales tax purposes. No sales tax should be applied to the amount of the coupon. The net result of using the coupon is it lowers the total sale price and the sales tax base. Retailers may offer discounts by issuing coupons that can only be redeemed in their business or by accepting manufacturer coupons for which they will be reimbursed by the manufacturer the amount of the discount from the coupon. When a customer uses a coupon, sales tax is applied to the final discounted price to the customer, after the coupon is applied to the sale. EXAMPLE: A restaurant issues a coupon for $5.00 off any two meals. Sales tax is added after the coupon is deducted from the price of the two meals. Cost of two meals: $20.00 Restaurant coupon: -5.00 Taxable receipt: $15.00 9.35% sales tax: 1.40 Customer payment: $16.40 Tips Tips are often a part of restaurant meals. When the tip is at the customer’s discretion, it is not a part of the restaurant’s charge for the meal and is not taxable. However, if the tip is mandatory, such as a statement on the menu, “An 18% gratuity will be added on parties of 6 or more,” the tip is subject to sales tax. Delivery, Freight, and Postage Beginning July 1, 2023, charges to the customer for handling and delivery are not subject to tax when separately stated on the invoice. To qualify for the exclusion from the sales tax base, delivery charges that are separately stated on the invoice, bill of sale, or similar document provided to the purchaser must be clearly denominated as charges for: (1) delivery; (2) transmission; or (3) transportation AND must be representative of a true or reasonable cost of delivery. Delivery charges may include shipping, postage, handling, crating and packing. EXAMPLE: A store sells a sofa bed to be delivered to the customer living in another county. The sales ticket for the sofa bed would read as follows: Cost of sofa $ 450.00 Delivery fee 45.00 Total $ 495.00 9.15% sales tax 41.18 Customer payment $ 536.18 NOTE: Freight and postage are taxable ONLY when they are not separately stated on the invoice and included as part of a total sale between the retailer and the customer. In the example above, if the customer contracted directly with a freight company to pick up and deliver the sofa, the freight company’s fee is not taxable. In either case, the sale would be sourced to the customer’s home address. Delivery charges for Direct Mail are not subject to sales tax when separately stated on the invoice. Direct mail means printed material delivered or distributed by the USPS or other delivery service to a mass audience or to addressees on a mailing list provided by the purchaser or at the direction of the purchaser when the cost of the items are not billed directly to the recipients. Direct mail includes tangible personal property supplied directly or indirectly by the purchaser to the direct mail seller for inclusion in the package containing the printed material. It does not include multiple items of printed material delivered to a single address. Discounts Common discounts are sale prices – 20% Off and Buy One, Get One Free offers. When a retailer discounts an item to the customer, sales tax is charged only on the amount actually paid by the customer. EXAMPLE: A grocery store offers a buy one, get one free sale on an item costing $2.59. Tax is charged on $2.59 – the sale price paid for both items. You may also offer your customers a discount for paying an invoice within a stated number of days. When a customer takes advantage of this type of discount, the sales tax is based on the discounted payment amount. EXAMPLE: You offer a discount of 2/10, net 30 on an invoice of $150 plus tax.
